PROFILE

Katsumi Okubo

Chairman, All Japan Seishinkai Karate Federation
Founder of ISKF · Soke of Seishinryu Kenpo Karate-do

Profile

Began studying karate in 1972. He trained with the karate club at Kotoni Technical High School and later studied Okinawan traditional martial arts at Tokai University, where he served as captain of the club in 1979.
In 1977, he was appointed by Soke Kikuchi as the youngest Seishinryu branch chief, taking charge of the Sapporo Branch.
In addition to karate, he studied Aikido during university. During his service in the Japan Ground Self-Defense Force, he also studied Jukendo (bayonet fighting), unarmed combat, and Jujutsu. He was awarded 3rd Dan by the Soke.
During his military service, he established the Seishinryu Engaru Branch and Rumoi Branch.

Following the death of his Soke, he reorganized the Sapporo Branch in 1986 and established the permanent training facility "Seishinkan Okubo Dojo."
Initially, he joined the Japan Seishinryu Karate-do Federation, headed by the former deputy head of the main dojo. He later left the organization and joined the Seishinryu Karate-do Association, an umbrella organization for Seishinryu groups, and continued his activities centered in Sapporo.
In 2004, the organization developed from a private local dojo into the Non-Profit Organization All Japan Seishinkai, which received certification from Hokkaido and was registered with the Legal Affairs Bureau.
As the number of branches grew, he established the All Japan Seishinkai Karate Federation. In 2011, following the establishment of its first overseas branch in Sri Lanka, he founded the international organization International Seishinryu Karate-do Federation (ISKF).
In 2012, he was awarded 10th Dan and the title of Soke (second-generation successor) by the association, becoming a completely independent organization.

He holds 8th Dan recognized by the National Protective Equipment Karate Federation, 8th Dan recognized by the Toko Association, and 10th Dan recognized by WUKO (World Union of Karate Organizations). He also holds the Japanese Chief Instructor qualification from FIKM and has received honorary 10th Dan from Seishito-ryu Sanshinkan International and the World Kobayashi-ryu Karate Kobudo Association (USA).
He later incorporated techniques and philosophies from the various styles he studied through his own training and named his school's style "Seishinryu Kenpo Karate-do."
Today, he serves as the Soke and Chairman of ISKF, which has more than 4,000 members across approximately 150 dojos in Japan and overseas.

Career History

Served at the Japan Ground Self-Defense Force bases in Engaru and Rumoi. After returning home, he joined Suzuki Jihan Hokkaido Co., Ltd., where he worked in sales, later serving as manager of the Ebetsu Sales Office and the company's East Sales Office. He eventually became head of the Sales Planning Group at the head office, where he was responsible for the company's transition to IT as well as events and advertising.
After retiring, he served as a director of the Toko Association Foundation. He currently operates a martial arts equipment store, Toyo Budo-gu, alongside his karate activities.

Organizations and Affiliations

Advisor, World Uechi-ryu Karate-do Federation , Chairman of IKMA, Honorary Member of Seishito-ryu Sanshinkan, and others.
He has also served as Advisor to the National Protective Equipment Karate Federation, Advisor to the Okinawa Kobayashi-ryu Karate Kobudo Federation, Director of the Hokkaido Mainichi Cultural Association, and Director of WRBBOF .
He has also served as the East Asia Regional Representative of WUKO-ad and Advisor to the Pan American Karate Federation.
